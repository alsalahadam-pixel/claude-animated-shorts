## Facts (D = documented in the sources below; R = reported by some accounts; I = illustrated: our staging, not a historical record)
- D: Tsutomu Yamaguchi (1916-2010), a ship designer (draftsman) for Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, from Nagasaki. Aged 29 in August 1945.
- D: He was finishing a three-month business trip to Hiroshima (from May 1945). 6 August 1945 was meant to be his last day.
- D: His wife Hisako and their infant son Katsutoshi were at home in Nagasaki.
- D: He realised he had forgotten his hanko (name stamp) and went back toward the shipyard.
- D: At 8:15 a.m. he saw a bomber and two small parachutes, then a great flash. He was about 3 km from ground zero.
  Burst eardrums and serious burns to the left side of his upper body. He spent the night in an air-raid shelter.
- D: He took a train home to Nagasaki and was treated and bandaged there.
- D: On 9 August, bandaged, he went to work. He was telling his supervisor about Hiroshima, and his supervisor did not
  believe that one bomb could destroy a city (Wikipedia: called him "crazy"). Then the same flash filled the room. In his
  own account the office was "blown over". Again about 3 km from ground zero.
- D: "I thought the mushroom cloud had followed me from Hiroshima." (The Independent, 2009). The narration paraphrases it as "He later said he thought...".
- D: His wife and son survived. R: they had taken shelter in a tunnel (accounts vary on the details).
- D: Deaths by the end of 1945: Hiroshima about 70,000-140,000, Nagasaki about 40,000-74,000 ("well over a hundred thousand").
- D: Late in life he spoke publicly about his experience (memoir, the 2006 documentary "Twice Survived" screened at the UN) and campaigned against nuclear weapons.
- D: On 24 March 2009 Japan officially recognised him as a survivor of both bombings, the only person so recognised.
- D: He died on 4 January 2010, aged 93.
- I: all scenes are illustrated. The exact rooms, the street, the train, the boss's face, the stamp on the certificate
  (a callback to the hanko), the tunnel reunion, the lanterns (a memorial image) and the bench under the blossom are our
  staging. The 3 km "map" is a diagram, not a real map. The end card says so: "An illustrated retelling of documented events."
- Removed from v1 because it was not documented: the boss's lines "that's crazy / no bomb could ever do that" as dialogue,
  the boss laughing, and "because of course he does".

## Script (v2, as narrated: 73.9 s of narration, 76.0 s cut)
This man is about to survive an atomic bomb...
For the second time.
Rewind three days.
August sixth, 1945.
Ship designer Tsutomu Yamaguchi is finishing a work trip in Hiroshima.
Today, he's finally going home, to his wife Hisako and their baby boy.
But he forgot his hanko, his name stamp, and turns back for it.
Eight fifteen.
A plane flies over, and two small parachutes drift down.
Then... the sky turns white.
Three kilometers away, the blast still bursts his eardrums and burns his left side.
He spends the night in a shelter, then catches a train home.
Home... is Nagasaki.
On August ninth, still in bandages, he goes back to work.
He tells his boss one bomb destroyed an entire city.
His boss doesn't believe him.
And right then... the same white light fills the room.
The blast throws him to the floor.
He later said he thought the mushroom cloud had followed him from Hiroshima.
And his wife and baby son were somewhere in that city.
They survived, sheltering in a tunnel.
But together, the two bombs killed well over a hundred thousand people.
Both times, he was about three kilometers from the center.
Close enough to be hurt. Far enough to live.
Late in life, he finally told the world his story.
In 2009, he became the only person officially recognized as surviving both bombs.
He died the following year, at ninety-three.
Two atomic bombs. Three days apart.
And the man who survived both spent his last years asking that there never be a third.
