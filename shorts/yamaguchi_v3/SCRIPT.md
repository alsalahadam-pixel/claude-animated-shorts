## Facts (D = documented, R = reported by some accounts, I = illustrated)
- D: Tsutomu Yamaguchi (1916-2010), ship designer (draftsman) for Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, from Nagasaki. He was in
  Hiroshima on a three-month business trip, and 6 August 1945 was to be his last day.
- D: His wife Hisako and their infant son Katsutoshi were at home in Nagasaki.
- D: He went back for his hanko (name stamp). About 8:15 a.m. he saw a bomber and two small parachutes, then the flash,
  about 3 km from ground zero. Ruptured eardrums and burns to the left side of his upper body. He spent the night in an
  air-raid shelter and took a train home the next day.
- D: On 9 August, bandaged, he went to work and was describing the Hiroshima blast to his supervisor, who did not
  believe it. Accounts say he was asked to report on Hiroshima. The flash came at about 11:02 a.m. He was again about 3 km away
  and was blown over ("the whole office... was blown over", his own account).
- D: "I thought the mushroom cloud had followed me from Hiroshima" (The Independent, 2009). Narrated as "He thought the mushroom cloud had followed him from Hiroshima."
- D: His wife had gone out to find burn ointment for him, and she and the baby sheltered in a tunnel (History.com,
  War History Online). Accounts vary on details. Narrated as "She'd gone out to find ointment for his burns, and sheltered with the baby in a tunnel."
- D: Deaths by the end of 1945: Hiroshima about 70,000-140,000, Nagasaki about 40,000-74,000 ("over a hundred thousand").
- D: He rarely spoke publicly about it for decades. After his son Katsutoshi died of cancer in 2005 (aged 59) he began
  speaking out, including at a UN screening of a 2006 documentary, calling for the abolition of nuclear weapons.
- D: In March 2009 (24 March) Nagasaki recorded his Hiroshima exposure on his survivor certificate, which until then listed only
  Nagasaki: the only person officially recognized as a survivor of both bombings. He died on 4 January 2010, aged 93.
- I: all scenes are illustrated. This covers the rooms, the street, the train, the faces, the boss's reaction, the stamp on the
  certificate (a callback to the hanko), the tunnel reunion, the lanterns (a memorial image), the bench, the diagram, and
  the 11:02 / 8:15 cards (the times are documented). The end card says: "An illustrated retelling of documented events."
- I/interpretation: "fighting so the world would never see a third" summarizes his documented campaigning against nuclear weapons.

## Script (V3, as narrated: Kokoro am_fenrir 1.45x, directed takes, 73.5 s of narration)
This man has already survived one atomic bomb.
Now, he's about to survive another.
How do you survive that... twice?
Hiroshima, August sixth, 1945.
He's a ship designer from Nagasaki, going home today to his wife and baby son.
But he's forgotten his name stamp, so he turns back.
Eight fifteen.
A plane.
Two small parachutes, drifting down.
Then... the sky turns white.
Three kilometers away, it still bursts his eardrums and burns his left side.
The next day, he takes the train home.
Home... is Nagasaki.
August ninth. Still bandaged, he's back at work,
telling his boss how one bomb destroyed an entire city.
His boss doesn't believe him.
And right then... the same white light fills the room.
The blast throws him to the floor.
He thought the mushroom cloud had followed him from Hiroshima.
And his wife and baby son were somewhere out there.
They were alive.
She'd gone out to find ointment for his burns, and sheltered with the baby in a tunnel.
Across both cities, over a hundred thousand others did not.
Both times, he was three kilometers away.
Close enough to be hurt.
Far enough to live.
For decades, he barely spoke of it.
Then cancer took his son, the baby from the tunnel.
So he began to speak out, even at the United Nations.
In 2009, he became the only person officially recognized as surviving both.
His name was Tsutomu Yamaguchi.
He died a year later, at ninety-three.
Two atomic bombs. Three days apart.
He survived both... and spent his last years fighting so the world would never see a third.
